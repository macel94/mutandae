# Brand decisions

## Decision

The product name is **Mutandae**.

The stylized visual and protocol mark is **μTandae**.

The canonical technical spelling is **`mutandae`**.

## Name forms

| Context | Form | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Company/product copy | Mutandae | Readable, searchable, and legally/practically usable as a word mark |
| Logo and visual identity | μTandae | Gives the brand a compact technical symbol and a distinctive internal transition |
| Protocol/product family | μTandae Protocol or Mutandae Protocol | Connects the protocol to the product without requiring Unicode in technical systems |
| GitHub repository | `mutandae` | Stable ASCII identifier |
| npm/PyPI/package names | `mutandae-*` | Avoids Unicode and normalization problems |
| URLs and API identifiers | `mutandae` | Reliable across browsers, tools, and integrations |

## Pronunciation

Read the product name as Latin, not as an English word:

> **moo-TAHN-dye**

The spelling is *mūtandae* for pronunciation guidance: the first two long vowels are approximated by “moo” and “tahn,” and the final Latin diphthong *ae* is approximated by “ai” or “eye.” Do not anglicize the final *ae* to an English “day” sound. This pronunciation should be used consistently in demos, presentations, documentation, and product introductions.

## Meaning and inspiration

The name draws on the Latin root *mutare*, associated with changing, altering, or moving. The form *mutandae* is an inflected gerundive form that can evoke “things that must be changed” or “things due for change.” That is a useful conceptual fit for credentials and identities that are due for renewal, rotation, correction, or retirement.

The visual identity may also draw inspiration from:

- **μ (mu)**: a compact scientific/technical symbol suitable for a lifecycle-control mark.
- **丹田 (tanden)**: a Japanese concept traditionally associated with a center where vitality or inner strength is gathered. 丹 also carries associations with cinnabar, vermilion, red pigment, essence, and elixir.

These are brand inspirations, not literal translations. `μTandae` is a creative visual construction, not a claim that the word is Japanese or that `Tandae` is the Japanese reading of 丹田. 丹田 is normally read *tanden*.

## Visual direction

The logo should explore:

- a stylized lowercase Greek μ as the primary silhouette;
- a controlled transition or renewal loop integrated into the μ or T;
- cinnabar/vermilion red as an accent rather than a full interface color;
- restrained technical typography;
- visual cues of rotation, renewal, and continuity rather than generic locks or vaults.

The brand should not rely on a literal lock, key, shield, or storage-vault icon as its primary metaphor. The product is a lifecycle control plane, not a generic secret container.

## Why not Mootandae?

`Mootandae` was considered and rejected:

- “moot” can mean debatable, irrelevant, or no longer actionable;
- the additional `o` weakens the connection to *mutare*;
- it is visually longer without adding product meaning;
- it does not materially eliminate the pronunciation similarity to Italian *mutande*.

## Why not μ丹田E?

`μ丹田E` was considered as a visual construction and rejected as the primary mark:

- it is difficult to type, search, pronounce, and reproduce;
- Japanese readers see 丹田 as *tanden*, not “tandae”;
- the final E requires an explanation that is not inherent in the word;
- it is better suited to an artful emblem than to a technical product identity.

The simpler `μTandae` keeps the distinctive visual direction while retaining a direct fallback to `Mutandae`.

## Clearance status

The name has undergone a provisional public-footprint screen:

- exact web searches found no meaningful existing software or company use;
- no exact GitHub repositories were found;
- no exact npm or PyPI package was found;
- no exact UK Companies House result was found;
- major domain variants appeared to have no RDAP registration record at the time of checking.

This does **not** establish legal availability. Before commercial launch:

1. Commission a professional trademark knockout search.
2. Search federal and international trademark databases for exact, phonetic, and confusingly similar marks.
3. Check the intended jurisdictions and relevant software/cloud/security classes.
4. Register the desired domains and social/developer namespaces.
5. Re-run clearance immediately before filing or public launch.
